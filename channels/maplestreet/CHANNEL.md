# Maple Street English: channel bible

Read this before writing anything for Maple Street English. It is the single source of truth for who the channel is for, how it sounds, who lives on the street, and how every episode is built.

## 1. Identity

| | |
|---|---|
| Channel | Maple Street English (@maplestreetenglish) |
| Tagline | everyday English, one street at a time |
| Promise | Every episode is one short, real-life scene on Maple Street. You hear it, learn 5-8 useful phrases, and hear it again, and now you understand all of it. |
| Format | Shorts 9:16 (35-55 s): one phrase or one mini-dialogue, 4-5 per week. Long 16:9 (6-9 min): one full episode, 1 per week |
| Language | English, US spelling, US accent for all main characters |
| Level | CEFR A2-B1 (every episode is labelled; A1 and B2 specials allowed when marked) |
| Visual style | cute sticker-style chibi characters (`character_style: chibi`) on the pastel theme `maple`; every character has a fixed colour and one signature item |
| Voices | one Kokoro voice per character (section 4) plus a narrator. The narrator speaks only in the lesson parts, never in the story |

## 2. Audience

- Adults and older teens (16+) learning English as a second language, worldwide: India, Indonesia, Vietnam, Philippines, Brazil, Mexico, Turkey, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, and others.
- Level A2-B1: they know the basics, understand slow, clear English, and freeze in real conversations.
- They want: phrases people actually say, natural speed, situations they will meet (shop, doctor, neighbors, work, travel).
- They hate: grammar lectures, childish content, teachers who talk for 5 minutes before teaching anything.
- Many watch on a phone with the sound low: **captions are always on and always match the audio word for word.**
- Not for kids: never aim content at under-13s, and do not mark videos as "made for kids".

## 3. Voice and tone

- Warm, a little funny, never silly. A sitcom with heart, not a classroom.
- **Story lines (dialogue):** natural spoken English with contractions ("I'm", "gonna" only from B1). Max ~12 words per line. Every line has a speaker tag (`MIA: Hi!`).
- **Lesson lines (narrator):** short and clear, max ~15 words, second person ("you"). Explain meaning with a simpler phrase, not grammar terms. Grammar names (present perfect...) only in the 16:9 "Grammar corner" segment, at most once per episode.
- Vocabulary: outside the target phrases, use only common words (roughly the 2,000 most frequent). If a harder word is needed, it becomes a target phrase.
- Never translate to another language inside the video (the audience speaks many languages). Explain with English, pictures, and context.
- No idioms or slang unless the idiom is that episode's target phrase.
- Numbers: prices and times are said as people say them ("three fifty", "a quarter past five"). Each video uses one style and the lesson explains it.
- **Endings: always the CTA line "See you next time on Maple Street!"**

## 6. Episode format

### 6a. Long 16:9 (6-9 min), the main episode

| Segment | Length | What happens | Who speaks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Cold open | 10-15 s | one funny or tense line from the story, with the level badge (A2) and place on screen | characters |
| 2. Title | 5 s | "Episode N · At the Bakery" + street map with the house highlighted | narrator (1 line) |
| 3. Story | 60-120 s | the full scene at natural speed, captions on | characters only |
| 4. Key phrases | 2-3 min | 5-8 phrases: phrase card → the line from the story → simple meaning → one more example | narrator + characters |
| 5. Grammar corner (optional) | 30-60 s | one pattern seen in the story, 2-3 examples, no tables | narrator |
| 6. Story again, slower | 60-150 s | same story at ~85% speed, target phrases highlighted in the captions | characters |
| 7. Your turn | 30-60 s | 3-4 lines: the character says a line (automatically slower), then a silent pause while the phrase stays on screen so the viewer can speak; **after every pause a short spoken praise** (`NARRATOR: Great job!`, "Nice! That sounds natural.", "Perfect!"), never the same one twice in a row; or the viewer answers a question as Mia, followed by praise | narrator + characters |
| 8. Quick quiz | 20-40 s | 3 questions (fill the gap / what would you say?), 3-second countdown, answer | narrator |
| 9. Outro | 10 s | a short teaser for the next house + CTA line | narrator |

16:9 videos have chapters (`chapter` on the first scene of segments 3, 4, 6, 7, 8).

### 6b. Shorts 9:16 (35-55 s), made from the main episode or standalone

Pick one pattern:
1. **"Don't say / Say"**: a wrong or textbook phrase vs. the natural one, shown in a 2-line mini-scene (e.g. "Give me a coffee" → "Can I get a coffee?").
2. **Mini-dialogue**: 4-6 lines at one place, then the key phrase card, then the dialogue again.
3. **"What does ... mean?"**: Mia hears a phrase, someone explains it with an example.
4. **Quiz Short**: one situation, 3 answer choices, countdown, answer + why.

Every Short ends with the place name and the CTA line, and links (in the pinned comment) to the full episode when there is one.

### 6c. Script format (for the scriptwriter)

```
[STORY]
MIA: Good morning! Something smells amazing in here.
WALTER: Morning! That's the cinnamon rolls. They just came out of the oven.
[LESSON]
NARRATOR: "Something smells amazing" is a friendly way to start a conversation.
```

- `[STORY]`, `[LESSON]`, `[REPEAT]`, `[QUIZ]` mark segments; every line starts with `NAME:` using the names in section 4 (in caps, first name only; Ms. Carter = `JUNE`, Dr. Park = `LENA`, Mr. Haddad = `OMAR`).
- `[REPEAT]` lines get an automatic silent pause after them.
- One sentence per line where possible.

## 8. Content pillars

1. Shopping and ordering (bakery, shop, prices, quantities)
2. Health and help (pharmacy, feeling sick, emergencies, advice)
3. Home and neighbors (renting, problems, invitations, small talk)
4. Getting around (directions, bus, time)
5. Social English (greetings, apologies, compliments, polite refusals)
6. "Real vs. textbook" English (how people actually say it)

## 10. Never

- Translations into any other language, flags, or "for Indonesians/for Indians" framing inside the video. (Titles stay in English.)
- Grammar tables, long rule explanations, or grammar names in Shorts.
- Phrases nobody uses in real life ("I am fine, thank you, and you?" as the only answer).
- Mocking accents, nationalities, religions, bodies, or mistakes. Mia's mistakes are treated kindly.
- Real brand names, real places, politics, religion, other creators.
- Medical advice: Dr. Park gives only everyday, general advice and says "see a doctor" for anything serious.
- Romance between characters (keep it family-friendly for a 13+ audience).
- Clickbait the episode does not answer.

- **Titles:** situation + benefit, max ~70 characters, with the level. Examples: "Ordering at a Bakery in English | Real Conversation (A2)", "Don't Say 'Give me a coffee' — Say This Instead".
- **Thumbnail:** the place's front + 1-2 characters with clear emotion, 2-5 big words with one highlighted word ("Order **Like a Local**"), a small level badge in the corner. Same layout every episode so the series is recognizable.
- **Pinned comment:** a practice question the viewer answers in English, ending with 👇 ("What would you order at Brooks Bakery? Write it as a full sentence 👇").
- **Description:** 2-3 sentences + the full list of target phrases with timestamps + "Full transcript" note. Hashtags are added automatically (#learnenglish #englishconversation #esl); add `#shorts` for Shorts.
